TL;DR

Recent World Action Models (WAMs) condition a separate action decoder on features taken from a single video denoising step — fast, but nothing ever trains those features to be good future representations. FRED makes that training explicit: the video model learns, by self-distillation, to predict from pure noise the representations a momentum teacher extracts from noisy ground-truth futures, and it gets a short observation history as temporal context.

Across real-world Franka and humanoid (OpenArm) experiments, FRED consistently beats competing WAMs, with the largest gains on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks under zero-shot environment generalization, and it turns human videos into robot skills: 50.0% success on tasks demonstrated only in human videos, with 97.2% instruction following.

Key insight

A single denoising step is a future representation generator

A video diffusion model cannot render a high-fidelity video in one step, yet its intermediate features from that one step capture the high-level semantics that matter for control — where the arm goes, where the object ends up. One-step WAMs work because the denoising objective implicitly teaches the model to produce these future representations. FRED's premise is that they should be trained explicitly.

Representation quality is measured by attentive probing on frozen video features: a lightweight probe classifies each clip into 15 motion-primitive classes (forward / left / close-gripper / …). Improving the representations the action decoder actually sees is what moves the success rate.
Method

FRED: future representation learning via self-distillation

We keep the decoupled WAM architecture — a large video diffusion model \(\mathbf{v}_\theta\) whose first layers \(\mathbf{f}_\theta\) produce an intermediate representation \(\mathbf{h}\), and a small diffusion-based action decoder \(\pi_\psi\) conditioned on \(\mathbf{h}\) and the proprioceptive state. Inference is one forward pass of the video model from pure noise \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\); no iterative denoising, no decoded video.

1

Targets from noisy futures

The teacher extracts \(\mathbf{h}^\tau = \mathbf{f}_\phi(\mathbf{o}^\tau_{t+1}\,|\,\mathbf{o}_{t-H:t},\mathbf{c})\) from a lightly corrupted real future (\(\tau=0.25\)). Moderate noise gives the most informative targets — better than clean frames or heavier corruption.

2

Self-distillation, DINO/iBOT-style

Patch-wise heads \(\mathbf{g}_\theta,\mathbf{g}_\phi\) turn \(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h}^\tau\) into softmax distributions; the teacher side is centered and sharpened to avoid collapse. The loss is a per-patch KL. No external encoder is needed — the teacher is the video model's own EMA.

3

History as temporal context

Both student and teacher are conditioned on \(H\) past frames rather than only the current one, giving motion cues that disambiguate what the future should look like.

Objective

$$\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{f}_\theta(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\,|\,\mathbf{o}_{t-H:t},\mathbf{c}),\qquad \mathbf{h}^\tau=\mathbf{f}_\phi(\mathbf{o}^\tau_{t+1}\,|\,\mathbf{o}_{t-H:t},\mathbf{c}),\qquad \mathbf{o}^\tau_{t+1}=(1-\tau)\,\mathbf{o}_{t+1}+\tau\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$$
$$\mathbf{p}=\mathrm{softmax}\!\big(\mathbf{g}_\theta(\mathbf{h})/T_1\big),\qquad \mathbf{p}^\tau=\mathrm{softmax}\!\big((\mathbf{g}_\phi(\mathbf{h}^\tau)-\mathbf{m})/T_2\big),\qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{KL}\big(\mathbf{p}^\tau_i\,\|\,\mathbf{p}_i\big)$$
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{video}}(\theta)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta)+\lambda_{\mathrm{SD}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\theta),\qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{action}}(\psi)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\psi),\qquad \phi\leftarrow m\,\phi+(1-m)\,\theta$$

The video model is trained by the video flow-matching loss plus self-distillation (\(\lambda_{\mathrm{SD}}=0.01\)); the action loss does not flow into the video backbone — stopping that gradient is better (68.8% vs. 65.6%). Features are taken from layer 18 of Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (layer 23 of Wan2.1-I2V-14B) for both self-distillation and action conditioning; the teacher EMA warms up to a decay of 0.9999.

Real-world results

Two questions, two robots

We test whether explicitly learned future representations help where WAMs are supposed to shine: (a) zero-shot generalization to a new environment after training on DROID with a Franka Research 3, and (b) human-to-robot knowledge transfer by co-training an OpenArm humanoid on robot demonstrations and action-free human videos.

Zero-shot environment generalization (DROID → new lab)

Four task suites, twelve trials each, no environment-specific data. FRED beats every one-step WAM at the 5B scale, and at 14B it beats DreamZero — a multi-step video-generating WAM on the same backbone — as well as the VLAs \(\pi_{0.5}\) and GR00T N1.7, with the biggest margins on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks.

Design choices

Teacher noise τ

0.00 → 65.3 · 0.25 → 68.8 · 0.50 → 67.8 · 0.75 → 67.6. Moderately noisy targets beat clean ones — diffusion features are most informative at intermediate noise.

Feature layer

L10 → 57.5 · L14 → 63.0 · L18 → 68.8 · L22 → 66.8 · L26 → 64.7. Intermediate layers carry the most control-relevant representation.

Loss coefficient λSD

0.1 → 68.1 · 0.01 → 68.8 · 0.001 → 62.7. A light self-distillation term is enough; too small and the effect vanishes.

Stop-gradient from actions

Letting the action loss update the video model: 65.6. Blocking it: 68.8. Optimizing future representations directly for action prediction is not the answer.

Appendix

Hardware & data

Franka Research 3 setup (DROID zero-shot evaluation)

Franka Research 3 with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, one wrist camera and left/right exterior cameras. Actions are relative joint angles (7D) plus absolute gripper opening (1D). Twelve trials per task with partial credit; 1,500 action steps for long-horizon tasks, 500 otherwise. The evaluation environment differs from DROID in location, background and objects.

OpenArm humanoid setup and the robot / human datasets

Two 7-DoF OpenArm arms with 6-DoF Inspire RH56F1 hands and a ZED 2i stereo camera on a 2-DoF neck; both egocentric views are used as input. Robot data is teleoperated with the ExoArm-7 exoskeleton and Manus Pro gloves.

Robot dataset: 553 demonstrations, 192K frames, 53 instructions over 4 skills and 6 object categories. Human dataset: 1,187 egocentric videos, 321K frames, 7 skills and 7 object categories — no action labels.

Training recipe (real-world experiments)

Wan2.2-TI2V-5B / Wan2.1-I2V-14B video backbones; 16- / 24-layer DiT action decoders (hidden 768 / 1024). Four historical frames + the current frame, eight predicted future frames (stride 3); action horizon 24. Self-distillation with \(T_1=0.1\), \(T_2=0.04\), \(\tau=0.25\), \(\lambda_{\mathrm{SD}}=0.01\); EMA teacher with \(1-(1+t)^{-3/4}\) warm-up. AdamW, cosine decay to 10% of the peak, 5K warm-up; batch 128 for Franka (150K / 100K iterations at 5B / 14B), batch 32 (+32 human) for OpenArm (50K iterations). Q01–Q99 normalization of actions and states. Four action denoising steps at inference.
