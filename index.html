Self-Distilling Future Video Representations for World Action Models

Anonymous authors
Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2027
Paper arXiv (soon) Code (soon)

TL; DR. We introduce FRED, a framework that trains World Action Models to generate better future video representations from a single denoising step, by self-distilling from a momentum teacher that processes noisy ground-truth futures and by conditioning on observation history.

Abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising approach to learning generalist robot policies using rich spatiotemporal priors from pre-trained video diffusion models. A recent line of WAMs improves inference efficiency by conditioning a separate action decoder on intermediate future video representations extracted from a single denoising step, avoiding the costly iterative denoising process required for video generation. This design makes action prediction depend directly on the quality of these future representations. In this paper, we introduce Future REpresentation learning via self-Distillation (FRED), a framework that trains WAMs to improve the quality of future video representations from noise in a single denoising step. Specifically, FRED uses self-distillation to predict future representations from noise, using representations extracted from ground-truth future observations as targets. We further condition the video model on historical observations, providing temporal context for predicting future representations better. Across diverse real-world manipulation scenarios, FRED consistently outperforms competing WAMs. It achieves particularly large gains on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks under zero-shot environment generalization, and substantially improves human-to-robot skill transfer, reaching 50.0% task success on tasks absent from the robot training data and demonstrated only in human videos, compared with 19.4% for the best competing WAM.

Method overview

FRED keeps the decoupled WAM architecture: a large video diffusion model $\mathbf{v}_\theta$ whose first layers $\mathbf{f}_\theta$ output the intermediate representation $\mathbf{h}$, and a small diffusion-based action decoder $\pi_\psi$ conditioned on $\mathbf{h}$ and the proprioceptive state. Inference is one forward pass of the video model from pure Gaussian noise $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$, with no iterative denoising and no decoded video. On top of this, FRED adds two ingredients.

Self-distillation from noisy futures. The student $\mathbf{f}_\theta$ generates $\mathbf{h}$ from noise, while a momentum teacher $\mathbf{f}_\phi$ processes a lightly corrupted ground-truth future observation $\mathbf{o}^\tau_{t+1} = (1-\tau)\,\mathbf{o}_{t+1} + \tau\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ under the same conditioning to produce the target $\mathbf{h}^\tau$:

$\mathbf{h} = \mathbf{f}_\theta(\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \mid \mathbf{o}_{t-H:t}, \mathbf{c}) \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{h}^\tau = \mathbf{f}_\phi(\mathbf{o}^\tau_{t+1} \mid \mathbf{o}_{t-H:t}, \mathbf{c})$

Patch-wise projection heads $\mathbf{g}_\theta, \mathbf{g}_\phi$ map both representations to distributions; following DINO and iBOT, the teacher logits are centered by their running mean $\mathbf{m}$ and sharpened with a lower temperature to prevent collapse. The self-distillation loss is a per-patch KL divergence:

$\mathbf{p} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\mathbf{g}_\theta(\mathbf{h}) / T_1\right), \quad \mathbf{p}^\tau = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left((\mathbf{g}_\phi(\mathbf{h}^\tau) - \mathbf{m}) / T_2\right), \quad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathrm{KL}\!\left(\mathbf{p}^\tau_i \,\|\, \mathbf{p}_i\right)$

History as temporal context. Both student and teacher are conditioned on a short history $\mathbf{o}_{t-H:t}$ rather than only the current frame, which provides motion cues (e.g., where the arm is heading) that disambiguate the future.

Training objective. The video model is trained with the video flow-matching loss plus self-distillation, and the action decoder with its own flow-matching loss. The action loss does not update the video model; the teacher is the EMA of the student, $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.

$\mathcal{L}_{\text{video}}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta) + \lambda_{\mathrm{SD}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\theta) \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{action}}(\psi) = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\psi)$

No external encoder is required: the teacher is the video model's own momentum copy, and the targets come from the robot data itself. By default we extract features from layer 18 of Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (layer 23 of Wan2.1-I2V-14B) for both self-distillation and action conditioning, use $\tau = 0.25$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{SD}} = 0.01$, and condition on four historical observations.

Real-world experiments

(a) Zero-shot environment generalization: train on DROID and evaluate in a new real-world environment on a Franka Research 3 without task-specific fine-tuning. (b) Human-robot co-training: train an OpenArm humanoid on robot demonstrations, optionally jointly with egocentric human videos, and evaluate on in-domain robot tasks and human-to-robot skill-transfer tasks.

Zero-shot environment generalization (DROID → new environment)

We evaluate four task suites with 12 trials each: in-domain easy tasks, an in-domain long-horizon task, out-of-domain tasks whose action verbs are absent from DROID, and object-grounding tasks with distractors of similar color or shape. FRED outperforms all one-step WAM baselines at the 5B scale, and at the 14B scale it outperforms DreamZero (a multi-step video-generating WAM on the same backbone) as well as the VLAs $\pi_{0.5}$ and GR00T N1.7, with the largest margins on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks.

Human-robot co-training (OpenArm humanoid)

Because video and action are separate modules, a WAM can be trained on human videos through the video objective alone, without action labels. We collect 553 robot demonstrations and 1,187 egocentric human videos on the OpenArm humanoid, and compare policies trained on robot data alone with those co-trained on robot and human data. FRED gives the best in-domain performance in both settings, and is the only WAM that reliably transfers skills demonstrated only in human videos: 50.0% task success and 97.2% instruction following, compared with 19.4% and 55.6% for the best competing WAM.

Analysis

A single denoising step is a future representation generator

Using DiT4DiT as the base WAM on RoboCasa Kitchen, task success is only weakly correlated with (a) one-step video generation quality ($r=0.24$) and (b) the quality of representations extracted from observed future videos ($r=0.49$), but strongly correlated with (c) the quality of future representations generated from noise in one step ($r=0.89$).

Recent WAMs achieve strong performance with a decoupled action decoder that conditions on intermediate features from a single video denoising step, which removes the iterative denoising of a large video backbone and makes WAMs practical for real-time control. Yet the underlying video diffusion models are never explicitly trained for one-step generation. We argue that single-step inference works because the denoising objective implicitly teaches the video model to generate future representations: it cannot render a high-fidelity video in one step, but its intermediate features already capture the high-level semantics that matter for control, such as global robot motion and target object placement. We therefore interpret the video diffusion model as a future representation generator, $\mathbf{h} = \mathbf{f}_\theta(\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \mid \mathbf{o}_t, \mathbf{c})$, and ask how to train it so that these representations become explicitly good for action generation. The figure above supports this view: what predicts task success is not one-step video quality nor representation quality on observed frames, but the quality of the future representations generated from noise, which is exactly the quantity the action decoder consumes.
